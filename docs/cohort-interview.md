# The five-user interview

Five conversations, half an hour each, before another parameter gets guessed.

Everything the roadmap has to decide next — how big a batch should be, what a
sensible default folder scheme looks like, what describing a library is worth —
is currently set by what felt reasonable on a test site with eighty files. The
opt-in counters on the overview page will eventually say what libraries look
like in aggregate. They will never say *why* somebody organised theirs the way
they did, and that is the half that decides what to build.

## How to use this

Send it as a message, or read it down the call. It is written to be sendable
as-is: no internal shorthand, no feature names they have not heard of, nothing
that reads as a survey.

Some ground rules that matter more than the questions:

- **Ask about last week, not about preferences.** "What did you do the last
  time you couldn't find an image" beats "how important is search to you". People
  are unreliable about what they want and reliable about what they did.
- **Do not describe the roadmap.** The moment somebody knows what you are hoping
  to hear, they help you hear it. Questions 8 and 9 are the only ones that
  mention anything that does not exist yet, and they come last for that reason.
- **Screenshots beat descriptions.** One picture of a real folder tree is worth
  the entire answer to question 4.
- **Let silence run.** The useful answer usually arrives after the polite one.

## The questions

**1. What made you install a media folders plugin in the first place?**
What were you trying to do that day? Was it folders you wanted, or something
else that folders looked like the answer to?

**2. How big is your library, roughly, and what is most of it?**
Hundreds of files or tens of thousands? Mostly photographs, mostly documents,
mostly product shots, a mix? If it is easy to check, the exact number is better
than the estimate.

**3. Who else uploads to it?**
Just you, or a team? How often does something new go in — daily, weekly, in
bursts when a project lands?

**4. How is it organised now? Could you send a screenshot of your folder tree?**
No tidying first — the messy version is the useful one. And what is the rule you
are following when you make a new folder: by client, by project, by date, by
what the file is, something else?

**5. When you cannot find a file, what do you actually do?**
Walk me through the last time. Did you search, click through folders, give up
and re-upload it, ask somebody?

**6. What do you type into the media search box?**
The literal words. Filenames? Something you remember about the picture? A client
name?

**7. Has your library ever got out of hand?**
Duplicates, files nobody can identify, things you are afraid to delete because
you do not know where they are used. What did you do about it — anything?

**8. If your library organised itself — files sorted into sensible folders,
every picture described and searchable by what is in it — what would that be
worth to you?**
Take it seriously as a purchase, not as a compliment. A number, or "nothing,
because…", are equally good answers.

**9. What would make you not trust it?**
Say the same tool could also tidy up: move files, merge duplicates, delete what
looks unused. What would it have to do — or refuse to do — before you would let
it touch your library?

**10. Anything about this you wish somebody would fix that I have not asked
about?**

## What to write down afterwards

Per person, in one place, within the hour:

- Library size, file-type mix, folder count and roughly how deep.
- The organising rule they described in question 4, in their words.
- The exact search strings from question 6.
- Their number from question 8, and whether they hesitated.
- Every condition from question 9. These are the requirements list for anything
  that mutates a library, and they are the reason Phase 0 ships a report that
  cannot delete anything.
- One sentence: what would this person be annoyed to still be doing next year?

Five of these, side by side, is the sample. It is small, and it is five more
libraries than the plugin has been designed against so far.
