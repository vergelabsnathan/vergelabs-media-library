## What is asked

An independent review of the plugin and the service, briefed with the surface
table so the whole thing is audited. Three questions of every way in, the
same three the surface's columns answer for each row:

- **can this caller do this at all** — the capability;
- **may they do it to _this_ object** — is the capability asked about the id
  in the request, or site-wide;
- **did they mean to** — the nonce.

And on top of those, the things a table cannot hold: a chain across two
routes, a race, a thing the model wrote that becomes markup, a path that gets
out of uploads, a host that is not ours, a secret in the wrong place.

What comes back: one row per finding in `docs/security-findings.md`, in that
file's shape. Every finding gets its own task with a proof and a commit that
names it; a row is `closed` only when the reviewer agrees it is.

## Scope

Copied from `SECURITY.md`, which is the published policy and the one the
review is held to.

In scope:

- The plugin, at any version wordpress.org or GitHub is serving.
- The AI service it talks to at `https://ai.vergelabs.nl/v1`.
- The account and licensing site at `https://vergelabsmedia.com`.

Out of scope, so nobody wastes an afternoon:

- Reports produced only by an automated scanner, with no working attack.
- Missing headers or version disclosure with no demonstrated impact.
- Anything that needs an administrator to already be compromised — an
  administrator can install any plugin they like, which ends every such chain.
- Denial of service by volume, and social engineering of our staff or users.

Two lines the policy states and the reviewer should hold us to: acknowledgement
within 24 hours by a person; a fix for anything remotely exploitable in hours.
`SECURITY.md` also says plainly what the plugin sends and where; that is the
data flow to check against.

## How to get a copy to attack

**The plugin, on your own site.** WordPress 6.5 or later on PHP 7.4 or later
(`readme.txt`).
The zip in `playground/vergelabs-media-library.zip` is the one served to the
world; it is rebuilt by `pnpm deploy:zip` and is what the commit above
contains. Or install from the source checkout as a plugin directory.

**WordPress Playground, in a browser, nothing to install.** Open
`playground/blueprint.json` on `playground.wordpress.net` (the
`?blueprint-url=` form pointing at the raw URL of that file). It installs the
zip from the repository's `main` and seeds demo data; it runs on PHP-wasm over
SQLite, so anything functional or destructive can be tried there and thrown
away. What it cannot show is performance or real MySQL. Against a working tree:
`pnpm play` mounts the checkout at `127.0.0.1:8899` (`docs/testing.md` has the
two Windows traps).

**A box with real MySQL.** The suites marked `box` in `tools/verify.mjs`
(`roles`, `paths`, `secrets`, and the rest of the battery) need one. The team's
own box is not shared; a box for the review, with the plugin deployed and the
suites runnable, is Nathan's to provide. `tools/deploy.mjs` refuses any host
not in its `BOXES` map, so a reviewer's box is added there, not typed in.

**The service.** `https://vergelabsmedia.com` (accounts, licences, Stripe) and
`https://ai.vergelabs.nl/v1` (what the plugin talks to) are live. A licence key
for the review, and whether it carries real credits, is Nathan's to hand over;
without a key the plugin makes no request at all, so the plugin-to-service
path needs one. The service's source, suites and migrations are in
`../service`; `pnpm test` runs them in-process against PGlite and touches
nothing live.

**What the plugin sends.** Stated in `SECURITY.md` and in `readme.txt` under
*External services*: nothing without a licence key and a run the user started;
each image downsized, never the original, with its file name, MIME type, site
address and the key; the key sealed at rest; describing refused on a staging
copy unless `VERGEML_AI_ALLOW_NONPROD` says otherwise.

## How to report

Findings to **security@in.vergelabs.nl**, as `SECURITY.md` says, or straight
into `docs/security-findings.md` in the shape that file states. A working
attack on the review copy is the standard; a scanner's list without one is out
of scope. Nothing here is under NDA and nothing needs to stay quiet after a
fix has shipped.
