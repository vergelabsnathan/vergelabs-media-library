# The plugin's AI, tested against our own six layers

Written 2026-09-08, against `What does this conclusion rest on` v6.3 EN — the
white paper this company publishes and asks vendors to be measured by. What
follows is the same TRACES test, turned around and applied to our own product,
and read from the code rather than from memory. Every claim below names the
file and the line.

The result in one line: **the plugin records what the model said and what the
library did, and does not record the link between them** — which is the exact
gap the white paper is about, in the product that sells the argument.

## SEAL, mapped onto this plugin

The white paper's four phases have a direct reading here.

| SEAL | in this plugin |
|---|---|
| **Source** | the picture, its page context (`core/seo-context.php`), the library summary the guide is handed |
| **Edit** | describe → `vergeml_ai_index`; the catalogue; `core/filing.php` scoring a picture against every folder |
| **Approve** | the draft on the Folders screen, and the Move the owner presses |
| **Launch** | the terms written to the library; `vergeml_librarian_moves` |

The white paper says the trail breaks at Edit — between the finding and the
recommendation. It breaks in exactly that place here.

## T · Trail

**What holds.** `vergeml_ai_index` is a real Trail layer for a description. Per
picture it records `caption, alt, tags, kind, has_people, has_text,
document_type, filing, orientation, embedding` alongside `model`,
`model_version`, `prompt_hash`, `described_at` and `error`. So for any
description the question "which model, which prompt version, when, and what
came back" has an answer without anyone having filled anything in — the white
paper's own test for a Trail that survives (§Trail: *"it writes along and
records as a byproduct of the work"*). `core/ai-index.php` also marks a field
as a person's the moment it is edited by hand (`locked`), and nothing the
pipeline writes paints over it afterwards.

`vergeml_librarian_batches` and `vergeml_librarian_moves` record that a move
happened: `batch_id`, `scheme`, `status`, `reason`, `attachment_id`,
`term_id`, `term_created`, `undone`, with timestamps. Every move is undoable
from that record.

**What is missing.** `vergeml_filing_pick()` in `core/filing.php:381` returns,
per picture:

```
'term_id', 'score', 'runner_up', 'runner_score', 'why', 'scores', 'gated'
```

`why` is one of `ok`, `floor`, `margin`, `gated` — the reason this picture went
where it went, or the reason it went nowhere. **Nothing stores any of it.**
There is no `update_post_meta`, no term meta and no table insert anywhere in
`core/filing.php`. The values are read once for on-screen text in
`core/folder-talk.php:1082` and `core/guide.php:1720`, and then discarded.

So the moves table can say *picture 1779 went into Architecture*. Nothing can
say *because it scored 0.81 against 0.44 for the runner-up, on the object
class, with the vector as tie-break, under prompt hash abc123*.

That is the white paper's sentence about a research report, unchanged: the
finding is not fabricated; the connection between finding and advice was never
recorded.

## R · Retrieval

**What holds, and it is the strongest thing in the codebase.**
`core/filing.php` **abstains**. The best folder has to clear a floor (0.55) and
beat the runner-up by a margin (0.08) or the picture stays where it is and the
run reports how many it left alone. The header explains the measurement it came
from: the old cosine-to-folder-phrase rule scored everything in a 0.28–0.44
band and was a coin flip. That is the Retrieval layer's hard requirement —
*"when the source is not there, the system says so instead of inventing
something"* — implemented, measured, and with the numbers written down.

**What breaks it.** The guide invents. `lib/guide.ts` in the service hands the
model a library summary and asks for a tree; the model then narrates per-folder
counts. On 4 September on the test box it produced *"Landscape and nature and
its five subfolders absorb most nature/scenery shots — roughly 220 combined"*,
*"Illustrations (23), Screenshots (6), Diagrams (5)"*, and *"all 641 images are
now routed"*. Nothing had run. Every number was generated by a text model that
had no way to compute it. The white paper's own example, verbatim: *"a sentence
that sounds as if calculation happened, without any calculation happening"*.

**And the negative question cannot be answered.** The white paper singles this
out: *"Which statement has no source, which recommendation leads back to
nothing: those are negative questions, and a system that only searches never
answers them."* Ask this plugin today which pictures sit in a folder that the
evidence does not support, and there is no way to answer, because `why` was
never stored. The information existed for the length of one function call.

## A · Approval

**What holds.** Nothing reaches the library without a person pressing Move. The
draft is inert; `lib/guide.ts:75` tells the model *"You never act on the
library. You only change the draft."* and the plugin, not the model, applies
it. Every batch is undoable.

**What is missing.** *"Somewhere it is recorded who approved which version."*
Nobody is recorded. `core/folder-talk.php:1748` holds `get_current_user_id()`
on a plan while it is in flight and checks it back at 1783, but the persisted
`vergeml_librarian_batches` and `vergeml_librarian_moves` carry no actor column
at all. After the fact, a site cannot say who filed 268 pictures, or against
which draft.

**And the approval is being undermined by a fabricated number.**
`js/vergeml-folders.js:599` turns a failed `guide/rules` request into
`{ rules: [], unfiled: 0, pictures: 0 }` and renders it. The scope radio at
line 619 then reads *"Move only the 0 unfiled pictures"* beside *"Move every
picture. Today's 19 folders are removed."* A request that failed presents the
safe option as pointless and the destructive one as the only one that does
anything. That is an approval step arguing for the wrong choice.

## C · Constraints

**What holds.** The model produces text and nothing else. Filing is arithmetic
in PHP. The move is a WordPress term write the plugin performs. The boundary is
technical, not a promise — the white paper's own distinction: *"A checkpoint
can be ignored. A boundary that is technically fixed cannot."*

**What is missing.** One action escapes it: **counting**. The counts a person
reads in the conversation are produced by the model, and the white paper names
that exact case as belonging outside the model. The plugin has the module —
`vergeml_filing_pick()` will say what the draft actually does to 641 pictures —
and does not call it before the model speaks.

## E · Expertise

**What holds.** The guide asks one question with two to four concrete choices
rather than guessing (rule 6), and it is required to give the number when it
refuses a folder ("only 8% name a colour"). Filing abstains rather than
guessing. The judgment stays with the owner: they answer, they press Move.

**Where it hollows out.** When the assistant says *"Done"* and *"nothing is
left unfiled"*, it is not leaving judgment with the owner, it is telling them
there is nothing left to judge. The owner asked *"did you put them in folder
yes or no?"* and was told *"Yes"*. The library still had 112 pictures in no
folder.

## S · Storage

Out of the plugin's hands and into the service's, and not assessed here. The
plugin's side of it is what leaves the site: `core/ai.php` sends the picture
and the page context, `core/seo-context.php` decides what context counts. The
white paper's questions — server location, retention, no training on customer
data, and all of it in the contract rather than in a sales sentence — are the
service's to answer and should be answered on the site, not in support email.

## The two numbers that started this

A side effect of the same gap, and the thing a customer sees first: the
dashboard says **116 not in folders** and the Folders screen says **112 in no
folder**. Both are arithmetically right. `vergeml_count_unassigned()` counts
every attachment with no folder; `vergeml_folders_facts()` counts over
`vergeml_ai_index` with `error = '' AND embedding IS NOT NULL`, so it only ever
sees described pictures. 645 attachments, 641 images, and the four
non-described files are the difference. Two populations, one phrase, no way for
the reader to tell — so the screens read as though the system disagrees with
itself.

## What this diagnosis does not say

It does not say the architecture is wrong. The split is right: the model
proposes, the deterministic matcher decides, the owner approves, the plugin
acts. Four of the six layers hold, and the abstain rule in `core/filing.php` is
a better answer to the Retrieval layer than most of what this white paper was
written about.

What is wrong is narrower and fixable: **the reasoning is computed and thrown
away, the model is allowed to be the source of numbers, and no record says who
approved what.** Three gaps, in that order of consequence.

The spec is `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-08-traces.md`.
